The Fab Line: 4 visual directions

The same screen (Station 01, AI in Production) in three looks. The palette comes from your outfit and illustration: moss and forest greens, bark brown, near-black, with the pale yellow of your skirt as the one accent. These are inspired by qualities (painterly cinematic landscapes, blocky earthy colours), not copied from any game's art, UI or characters. Fonts here are system stand-ins. Contrast was checked: all text ≥4.5:1, and brown is used for fills and borders only, never for text.

A · Painted Highlands

Cinematic and painterly: a wind-swept hillside painted behind the UI, ink-brush strokes, a minimal thin-line HUD, and one bold accent (your skirt yellow) that pulls the eye to numbers and the Résumé. It matches your Ghibli portrait best. Calm, confident, a little poetic.

B · Block Biome

Blocky and earthy: grass-topped card headers, chunky bevelled buttons, square everything, a stepped horizon and a segmented XP bar. It's playful and instantly reads as "game", and pixel art for your sprite would fit naturally. The trade-off: it clashes with the soft Ghibli portrait, and it reads younger and less senior.

C · Painted Sky, Block Ground

A hybrid: the cinematic painted landscape from A behind everything, with chunky grass-and-dirt card frames and bevelled buttons from B in the foreground. The world feels cinematic, and the UI feels like a game. It keeps your portrait at home and still gets the earthy, blocky colour story.

D · Dusk over the Hills

Painterly and cinematic like A, but at golden-to-blue hour: a sky that fades from deep blue to pink over moss-green hills. Pink is the accent (Résumé, numbers), blue carries the diagrams, green means "completed" and gold is saved for achievements. It's the most emotional and memorable of the four, and still sits well beside your watercolour portrait.
