About

// whoami

I'm a Nanotechnology Engineering student at the University of Waterloo, specializing in nanoelectronics. I went into engineering wanting to build physical hardware, and my coursework sits where physics meets fabrication: quantum mechanics, microfabrication, polymer science and continuum mechanics.

My work terms pulled me the other way, into software and applied AI. At Communitech I built and shipped an agentic AI system that monitors the Waterloo tech ecosystem; at The Plug Co I built an AI-integrated backend with vector search; and on the Battery Workforce Challenge I wrote state-of-charge algorithms and embedded C diagnostics for an EV battery pack.

Outside work I build open-source engineering projects end to end — an FPGA place-and-route tool and an ML kernel compiler in C++, EV control software in C99, a thin-film lab toolkit, and ML systems in Python and Go — each with tests, CI and honest benchmarks.

That mix is the point: I like problems where the device, the data and the code all matter. Long term, I want to work on the hardware behind quantum computers.

PRJ-002 · CAPSTONE · GROUP 11DESIGN PROPOSAL

Wearable Patch for Doxorubicin Monitoring in Breast Cancer Patients

NE Capstone Design · 4-person team

A minimally invasive patch that uses hydrogel microneedles and an electrochemical sensor to track a chemotherapy patient's actual doxorubicin exposure, so clinicians can spot patients at higher risk of heart damage. Design proposal presented Nov 24, 2026; build and testing run Sep 2027 – Apr 2028.

  • Problem: the dose is set by body size, but patients clear the drug at different rates, and blood work and heart imaging catch cardiotoxicity only after it happens.
  • Design: swellable PEG hydrogel microneedles draw interstitial fluid up to a screen-printed carbon electrode, keeping sampling and sensing separate. Chosen over hollow-needle microfluidics and cleanroom-made carbon microneedles.
  • Sensing: doxorubicin is redox-active, so square-wave voltammetry reads it with no labels; peak current scales with concentration and is converted through a calibration line.
  • My role: electronics and readout: detection method, portable potentiostat, system architecture, power and electronics budgets.

MY PARTElectronics & readout systemARCHITECTURE · POWER · LAYOUT · BUDGET›
  • Disposable cartridge + reusable pod: only the sterile cartridge (microneedles, electrode, adhesive) touches skin. The sealed, wipeable pod is loaned by the clinic to one patient for the whole course and covered by a single-use polyurethane film, with no open ports. Chosen over a fully disposable design (like Dexcom G7 / FreeStyle Libre 3) for hygiene, accessibility and cost.
  • Signal chain: AD5940 single-chip potentiostat (50 pA – 3 mA) → nRF52840 MCU that schedules a scan every 10 min, applies batch calibration and sends concentration over Bluetooth LE; NFC tap-to-pair, LED + vibration alerts for patients without a smartphone, on-chip flash when no phone is nearby.
  • Power budget: sleeps between ~20 s scans; estimated Iavg ≈ 0.24 mA from datasheet values. C = Iavg·t / η = (0.24 mA)(24 h) / 0.7 ≈ 8.2 mAh, so the 40 mAh LiPo has ~5× margin and still exceeds 2 days at double the current.
  • nA-level signals: short traces, guard ring on the working-electrode trace, analog routing away from the antenna, no radio during scans, firmware baseline subtraction and temperature correction; to be validated against a lab potentiostat on identical samples.
  • Budget call: skipped the ~$611 AD5940 evaluation kit (~40% of the budget) in favour of a custom board from Analog Devices' reference design plus a borrowed lab potentiostat; electronics come to ~$250 per unit set.
